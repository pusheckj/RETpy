## [Unreleased]

### Added (assumption presets)
- Assumptions: two buttons at the top, **All conservative assumptions** and **All typical assumptions**. One click sets 26 assumptions for you: the return set, stock mix, glide path, fee, inflation, plan-until ages, Social Security share paid from 2033, success target, spending guardrails, long-term care (cost and yearly chance), spend-less-later, salary growth, Rule of 55 and the Roth ladder. You no longer need to know good numbers
- The presets read your situation. A longer retirement gets a bond tent, a higher success target and a lower-return set. A short one eases into bonds. Couples get a longer plan-until age and double the care chance. A loan keeps conservative inflation at 2.5%. Younger people get a lower Social Security share. Early retirees get the Roth ladder (and the Rule of 55 for typical). Salary growth is left alone if you edited your pay table. Rules and sources: `docs/METHODOLOGY.md`, section 15.3
- A **What changed** box lists each old and new value, with a "Why these numbers?" list. **Undo** puts the old values back exactly, until you edit one of them. The status line says Typical, Conservative, Custom (naming what you changed) or "set for your old plan" when your retirement age or household changed. It compares your settings with both presets, so it works after Remember me, a reload or a profile load
- Review fixes: the Pessimistic reason now says stocks grow about 2.5% a year after inflation (5% average with 22% swings), not 1%. Switching to Just me no longer shows a false "Custom" line, and a second click on the same preset keeps the first Undo. The "What changed" line names both ages for a couple with a younger partner so the years add up. A partner's salary growth is always set (only your own pay table can block it). A loaded profile counts as hand-edited only when its pay table differs from the built one
- The section is now titled **Assumptions** (was Assumptions (advanced)). Investments, Returns and mix points to it
- New `ui/utils/assumption_presets.py` (pure rules and callbacks) and `ui/components/assumption_presets.py`. `assume_` is added to `widget_sync._EXTRA_INPUT_PREFIXES`. No engine change: every preset key already existed in `retirement_sim.config`

### Added (results headline: ways to reach your goal, success band, cushion)
- Retirement tile, under the hero number: **Ways to reach 90%** (or your own target while you are below it; else the next step, 90% then 95%). Rows: retire later (the smallest later age that reaches the goal, plus the one just short of it when close), spend 5-25% less in retirement, your partner works up to 5 years longer (couples), and one year later plus the smallest spending cut. Each row is its own simulation at the main run's count (capped at 2,000) on the main run's market paths, so keeping a change gives exactly the rate shown. Rows that reach the goal are marked; the others show their real value
- **Try it** previews a change without touching your inputs. **Keep this change** writes it into your inputs, updates the side panel and reruns the plan. **Undo** puts the old values back
- A plan already at 95% or more shows **You have room** instead: the earliest age that still meets your target, and the most extra retirement spending (+5/+10/+15%) that does
- The levers run after the main result, like the other follow-up analyses (`utils/goal_levers.py`, `plan_goal_levers`: on the server, its fork pool or in the browser), and are cached by your inputs. A light placeholder shows while they compute
- The hero card now explains the success rate: a band (Needs attention under 65%, Fair 65-80%, Good 80-90%, Strong 90% and up; thresholds on hover), a count ("737 of 1,000 simulated markets last until your partner turns 95"), and the spending-guardrail cushion: how far the typical balance at retirement can fall before fun spending is trimmed, and by about how much a month. Read from your guardrail settings; with dynamic spending off it says spending never adjusts

### Added (income Sankey and portfolio milestones)
- Income tile: under the *Over time* chart, a second chart, **Where this year's pay goes** (both show at once). The new chart is a Sankey of one working year in today's dollars: you and your partner's pay flow into taxes (federal, state, Social Security + Medicare), pre-tax savings (401(k)s, HSA) and take-home pay. Take-home flows into spending (by expense category), Roth IRAs, after-tax 401(k), 529 plans and brokerage (what's left). Pick the year with the age slider. Hover shows dollars and the share of gross pay. A caption gives the share of pay saved and the employer money (401(k) match, HSA deposit, ESPP discount) added on top. On a phone it is the short version: pay to taxes, saving and spending
- The numbers are the engine's: `sim_runner.working_year_flows` uses `working_year_cashflow` for pay, taxes and take-home, and replays the working years to take that year's spending from the engine's own spending log (housing timeline, children, college after the 529s, debt payments included). When spending and saving need more than take-home, the gap shows as *Shortfall from savings*, never as a negative brokerage amount
- Investable portfolio range chart: a third view, **Range + milestones**. A median line with 25-75% and 10-90% bands, and labelled markers from your inputs: your and your partner's retirement (or *Both retire*), mortgage paid off, the children's college years (shaded), 59½, each Social Security claim, RMD start, debts paid off and plan end. Hover a marker for what changes that year. Labels stagger into rows so none overlap, and all use one text size. The axis stops at 1.6 times the 75th percentile's peak when a few lucky paths would flatten the median (the caption says so). Today's $ / Future $ works here too. *Box plot* stays the default

### Added (Deep dives: historical replay and Roth conversion optimizer)
- Stress tests: **If you had retired in a real year**. A button replays your plan through real market history, retiring at your target age in each start year whose whole plan fits in 1926-2024. It shows "N of M start years last to the end" beside the simulation's success rate, the hardest start periods (found from the results), a year grid by decade (✓ lasted / ✕ ran out at age N; hover or tab for details; five years per row on a phone) and a table. Runs in the browser when it can (a `sim_runner` plan)
- Engine: `run_detailed_simulation(retirement_age, historical_start_year=Y)` replays real stock and bond returns from `historical_returns.py` (real, so no deflating), less fees, at the plan's mix. Working years use the years before `Y`. Years outside 1926-2024 earn the expected median return with no randomness. Deterministic; the existing `historical_sequence=` mode is unchanged. New `historical_replay_years`, `historical_replay_return`, and the `mc_jobs` calls `replay` and `outcomes`
- New Deep dives tab **Roth conversions**. A button compares no conversions and "fill the 12% / 22% / 24% bracket" with a yearly cap of $50K / $100K / $200K, plus your own setting, on the same simulated markets. A teal heat map shows each plan's success and median ending balance, outlines the best, and marks plans within simulation noise of it (2 paired standard errors) as "≈ best". **Use best** writes the setting into your inputs (with **Undo**); **Preview best as a scenario** pins it next to your current plan in Scenario comparison. The table view adds median taxes paid in retirement
- Investments, Roth conversion: the "Fill up to income of" box now says it is income before the standard deduction, and which bracket the amount reaches (the default $100,800 joint is "partway into the 12% bracket"). Saved profiles behave as before

### Added (bond tent and forward-looking returns)
- Retirement, Social Security: new **Share of benefits paid from 2033** slider (50-100%, default 100%). The trust fund is projected to run short around 2033; about 78% models no fix. It scales both people's benefits and the spousal top-up from that year (`SS_PAYABLE_FRACTION`, `SS_PAYABLE_FROM_YEAR`, `config.ss_payable_factor`). Default 100% changes nothing
- Investments, Glide path: a new **Shape** choice. *Ease into bonds* is the old behavior. *Bond tent* holds the most bonds in the years around retirement: stocks step down over the 5 years before you retire, then climb back over the years you choose (3-25, default 10) to a share you choose. A crash in those years does the most damage, and later stocks grow the money left over. The stock-share sliders now go to 30-100% (at retirement) and 20-100% (end). New config: `GLIDE_PATH_SHAPE`, `GLIDE_PATH_TENT_RAMP_YEARS`, `GLIDE_PATH_TENT_RISE_YEARS`. The working years now read their mix from `calculate_asset_allocation` too; for *Ease into bonds* that is still `STOCK_ALLOCATION`, so results are unchanged
- Return scenario: new **Forward-looking** choice, from investment firms' November 2026 forecasts for the next 10-15 years (base case): stocks 5.1% (3.9% after the ups and downs), bonds 2.4%, volatility 15.5% / 6%, stock-bond correlation +0.10. It is also in the What if curve, the net-worth projection and the FAQ. The scenario's own correlation applies only while it is active

### Fixed (volatility drag was counted twice)
- The Monte Carlo drew ARITHMETIC yearly returns (the scenario means, e.g. 8.7% stocks Historical) and compounded them, which already produces the volatility drag. It then subtracted half the portfolio variance from every draw as well, so the drag was counted twice. 100% stocks at 8.7% / 19% compounded at a median of about 5.3% real instead of about 7.0% (30-year median, 20,000 paths); 80/20 at about 5.4% instead of 6.6%. The return-scenario Monte Carlo (`generate_correlated_returns_for_scenario`) had the same double count, per asset
- The engine now draws arithmetic returns and subtracts nothing. Deterministic projections that use one expected rate (net-worth projection, Debt payoff, Freedom milestones / Coast FIRE) use the geometric rate from the new `config.geometric_portfolio_return`: arithmetic mean less fee less half the blended portfolio variance (with correlation), computed in one place. That rate about matches the simulation's median growth. Freedom milestones now follow your stock allocation too (previously they read the default 80/20 table)
- Results improve. Default config: earliest age 54 to 51, success at 55 86% to 95%, median final portfolio $6.2M to $13.5M (1,000 runs). Mid-career couple preset: earliest 48 to 46. Median US 25-year-old: 46 to 41
- `APPLY_VOLATILITY_DRAG` no longer does anything. The "Lower returns for volatility" toggle is gone from Investments. Saved profiles that carry the key (on or off) still load and run the same as without it. The return-scenario help and the FAQ now say the scenario returns are averages and that compounding grows a little slower (about 6.3% for 80/20 Historical)

### Fixed (employer HSA deposit and stale Roth-conversion targets)
- The engine ignored your employer's HSA deposit (`YOUR_HSA_MATCH`): the simulated HSA got only your own amount, while the savings table showed both. The deposit now goes into the HSA every working year. It is not paid from take-home and is not federal income or FICA wages. California and New Jersey tax it as they tax your own HSA contributions (`working_year_taxes(..., your_hsa_employer=)`). With `YOUR_HSA_MATCH = 0` results are unchanged
- The IRS HSA limit covers your amount and your employer's together. Your amount is now capped at the limit less the employer's, and the employer's at the limit. A set amount at today's room still means "the max" and follows the limit as you age (the 55+ catch-up included)
- Max out for the HSA sets your amount to the limit less your employer's deposit. It used to overfund by that amount. Auto-allocate, Custom %, the sidebar tax and the net-worth projection use the same figures
- A warning now shows next to the HSA input when your amount plus your employer's is over the limit ("Your $8,300 plus $750 from your employer is $300 over the $8,750 limit. The plan uses $8,000."). The Money check-up warns when its HSA total is over the limit
- New Investments input: "Your employer's HSA deposit ($/yr)" (`YOUR_HSA_MATCH`)
- A saved Roth-conversion target equal to a 2025 default (12% bracket top: joint 96,950, single 48,475, head of household 64,850) now follows the default like the 2024 ones did, so it uses the 2026 table. Any other amount is kept

### Fixed (hosted visitor safety)
- An uploaded spending rules file can no longer freeze the shared server. Every pattern in an uploaded rules file is now plain text: a case-insensitive "contains" test, with no regular-expression syntax at all, so matching time grows only with the text length. Write `gadget.com/us`, not `GADGET\.COM/US`. The bundled default rules and the rules file in your own spending folder keep full regex syntax and behave as before. The Spending view help and the FAQ say so
- Uploaded rules are checked before use. Each pattern must be a non-empty text value of at most 200 characters. At most 500 patterns and 20,000 pattern characters in total. Category and label values, names, `fixed_monthly` items (at most 50) and `work_trip_window_days` (0 to 31) are type-checked too. A list or object where text belongs is a clean "Using the default rules" message, never a crash
- The "Simulation cache" panel (Recompute everything) is not drawn when hosted. It cleared caches shared by every visitor's session

### Fixed (Social Security tax and excess health premium)
- Social Security taxability now counts the year's realized capital gains in provisional income (both the provisional quote and the true-up). Before, only ordinary income was passed, so a year with gains under-taxed Social Security (40,000 of benefits plus 60,000 of gains on a joint return: 34,000 taxable, was 6,000)
- When the true health premium is below the provisional one that sized the withdrawals, the part of the difference that Pass 1 really delivered as cash goes back into taxable brokerage and counts as reinvested surplus. Before it left the portfolio without being spent. Nothing is returned for a Pass-1 shortfall, so no money is created. The withdrawal log drops the returned cash from the year's withdrawals (pro rata across sources, gross of tax) and no longer takes it off the RMD figure

### Fixed (sim_runner: shared cache, pool, config and error reporting)
- Results that include a visitor's browser replies are no longer written to the shared (or disk) cache, so a forged reply cannot be served to later visitors with the same inputs. `finish_plan(..., from_browser=True)` skips `plan.store`; `browser_compute` sets it for any job filled from a browser reply
- A hung pool worker no longer freezes a run: the pool is not joined on exit, its workers are terminated at the 300 s deadline, and the same seeded calls run serially
- A bad override (for example a text `YOUR_AGE`) no longer leaves the shared config half written: `_write_overrides` puts the originals back before it re-raises
- Engine errors are reported: `primary_result["sim_errors"]` / `["sim_first_error"]`, and `scan_sim_errors` / `scan_sim_first_error` for the age scan. The success-rate maths is unchanged
- `primary_result["trajectory_is_synthetic"]` is True when no real path was available and the placeholder curve was used; the first trajectory failure is logged
- "Recompute everything" now also clears the mortgage paydown cache (all small caches are in one list), and a disk hit no longer grows a small cache past its bound
- Changing only the target retirement age reuses the success-by-age scans (their own cache key) and reruns only the target-age batch; results equal a cold run

### Changed (one random seed per simulated path)
- Every Monte Carlo path now restarts its own random stream: path `i` of a run is seeded `mc_jobs.PATH_SEED + i` (`account_simulation.path_seed`). Before, the target-age run used one stream per chunk (seed 2000 + chunk), so an input change that altered one path's draw count moved every later path in its chunk onto new numbers. Applies to the target-age run (`mc_parallel.sim_task`), the place check and mortgage paydown sweeps that reuse it, the FI number sweep, the safe-withdrawal sweep (its `mc`, `w1` and investable-balance calls), `run_monte_carlo_detailed` / `_networth` / `_with_scenario` when given `random_seed`, and the legacy fallback. Sensitivity, stress presets and the what-if baseline run through the full analysis, so they follow
- The FI and safe-withdrawal sweeps used a different seed per grid point (3000 + i, 5000 + i); every point now replays the same paths, like the age scan. The target-age run and the age scan share their paths too
- Results no longer depend on how a run is split into chunks; the paths kept for the charts are the run's first ones
- Mid-career couple (side panel drawn), retiring at 48, 1,000 runs, spending in $200-a-year steps over 21 points: largest step 2.3 to 0.6 points; steps where more spending raised success 9 to 2 (each +0.1 point). In $200-a-month steps: largest step 4.7 to 3.2 points. Run time unchanged (target-age run 3.45 s with the pool, 11.0 s serial; full analysis 5.1 s)
- Results move by sampling noise only: default sample earliest age 46 (both), success at 60 95.4% to 96.8%; Young professional 42 (both), 99.7% to 99.5% at 55; Mid-career 48 (both), 97.9% at 55 (both); Near retirement 55 (both), 100% at 60

### Fixed (success-rate spike on small withdrawals)
- A working or retirement year that needed less than $100 from the portfolio could fail the whole plan. The tax gross-up stops with up to $1 still owed, and the 99% check counted that as a shortfall. In the Mid-career preset (as a bare dict, before the pay table is drawn) a $52.51 working-year deficit delivered $51.96 and failed a $2.25M household. Every path had the same deficit, so success at 55 fell from 61.7% to 37.3% for $200 more spending a year (1,000 runs, common seeds) and came back at the next $200. The check now allows the solver's $1 slack (`_fell_short`); needs of $100 or more are judged exactly as before
- The 2024-vs-2026 jump in the Mid-career preset (41.1% to 63.4%) was this spike: on the 2024 tables the preset sat on it. With the fix the 2024 tables give 61.5%

### Changed (2026 tax figures, verified)
- All tax figures checked against primary sources in November 2026; the report (quotes, links, changed / confirmed) is `docs/data_sources/2026_verification.md`, linked from `docs/TAX_MODEL.md`. Every item marked changed is applied; confirmed items (contribution limits, wage base, earnings test, Roth and student-loan phase-outs, child tax credit, NIIT, Additional Medicare Tax, Social Security taxation, poverty guidelines) are unchanged
- Federal 2026 (Rev. Proc. 2025-32): ordinary brackets for single, joint and head of household (head of household has its own 24% / 32% tops); standard deduction $32,200 / $16,100 / $24,150 (was 2024's $29,200 / $14,600 / $21,900); 65+ add-on $1,650 / $2,050 (from the Rev. Proc., not the report); LTCG 0% tops $98,900 / $49,450 / $66,200. The Roth conversion target (top of the 12% bracket) follows: $100,800 / $50,400 / $67,450, and a saved 2024 default follows the status. `STANDARD_DEDUCTION_*_2024` names stay as aliases
- New OBBBA senior deduction: $6,000 per person 65+, cut by 6% of MAGI above $75,000 single / $150,000 joint, tax years 2025-2028 (plan years whose calendar year is 2028 or earlier). Not indexed, so it shrinks in today's dollars. Working years and retirement years; in retirement the phase-out is applied at the MAGI before and after each income. Switch: `MODEL_SENIOR_DEDUCTION`
- Medicare 2026 (CMS): standard Part B $202.90, IRMAA tiers above $218,000 joint / $109,000 individual, 2026 Part B and Part D surcharges. Each enrollee also pays an estimated $200 a month for a Part D plan and Medigap (`MEDICARE_SUPPLEMENTAL_MONTHLY_PER_PERSON`; an estimate, not CMS). The old $400 a month for a couple was below two Part B premiums and was never read by the engine; the reference figure is now $805.80. At 65-74 premiums ($4,835 a person) now exceed the 'medicare_base' band share ($4,000) and set the cost. The tier still uses this year's MAGI, not the two-year lookback
- ACA 2026 (Rev. Proc. 2025-25): applicable percentage bands with the step at 133% (2.10% below, 3.14% to 4.19% from 133% to 150%; the age-rated model had 2.10% to 4.19%), linear inside each band, 9.96% at 300-400%, no credit at 400% (the enhanced credits expired at the end of 2025; an extension had passed the House, not the Senate). The legacy flat-band path uses the same table (was 4% / 6.5% / 9.5%) and the 2025 guideline for a family of four ($32,150). Benchmark premium for a 40-year-old $625 (KFF US average; was $650)
- California 2026 (FTB): brackets for single, joint and head of household; standard deduction $5,900 / $11,800 / $11,800; exemption credits (personal $158 each, senior $158 per person 65+, dependent $491 per child under 17), phased out above estimated 2026 AGI thresholds; the 1% Behavioral Health Services Tax above $1M of taxable income is now a separate surtax, not a tenth bracket. New optional `exemption_credits` / `surtax` fields in `state_tax_data.py`, applied by `config.state_income_tax` in the engine and the sidebar alike. Other states are still 2024
- Results, 1,000 runs, each preset's own target age: default sample (25-year-old couple) earliest age 51 to 50, success at 60 96.7% to 97.9%; Mid-career couple success at 55 41.1% to 63.4%, earliest age none by 75 to 58; Near retirement earliest 55 both, success at 60 99.9% to 100%. Lower federal and California taxes raise working-year savings; the Medicare change alone lowers Mid-career success by about half a point. The Mid-career jump is mostly the engine's sensitivity: on the 2024 tables, $1,200 a year less spending also moves it from 42.8% to 66.2%
- Docs and app text now say 2026 for the federal tables (and the state table's own year), the FAQ's healthcare answer gives the 2026 Part B premium and the 400% cliff

### Changed (Freedom milestones: sourced place costs)
- Freedom milestones places now use sourced cost-of-living data (November 2026) instead of rough guesses: BEA Regional Price Parities 2024 (renormalized, US = 1.00), KFF 2026 benchmark premiums for health, Zillow home values (September / October 2026) and single-family rents (October 2026; a state's rent is modeled from its metros), and property tax a new buyer pays plus Insurance.com 2026 insurance. Places abroad use Numbeo estimates (November 2026, ECB rates of 13 November). Sources and caveats: `docs/data_sources/2026_verification.md`. The "How this is calculated" text and help lines say so.
- Abroad cities now match the data: Italy is Bologna, Spain is Valencia, Mexico is Mérida (home price is a house), Portugal is the Lisbon area (city price), Thailand is Chiang Mai. Abroad health uses about age-40 premiums, so it understates the cost at 60 and over.
- The property-tax-and-insurance rate of a bought home is higher: US 2.0% (was 1.3%), Florida 4.2% (insurance), California 1.7% (a new buyer's tax resets). It includes insurance, replaces the plan's own home carrying cost and is never added to it. Health ratios between places moved too, e.g. Vermont, West Virginia, Wyoming and Alaska now cost 1.6-2.1x the US benchmark. Everyday cost ratios between areas narrowed a little (San Francisco Bay Area to Ohio: 0.83x to 0.88x).
- New data sanity test (`tests/test_place_costs_data.py`: 76 areas, plausible bounds, every state present, codes unique, US row = 1.00).

### Fixed (remaining model gaps)
- Social Security earnings test: a claimant under full retirement age who still earns wages has $1 withheld per $2 above $24,480 (years before the full-retirement-age year) and $1 per $3 above $65,160 in that year (2026 limits, flat in today's dollars). Each person is tested on their own pay. From full retirement age the benefit is recomputed as if claimed later by the months withheld (`account_simulation.social_security_for_year`; fractional months).
